# Project Atlas — Resource Management Plan

## 1. Purpose

This Resource Management Plan defines how Project Atlas will identify, acquire, organize, allocate, manage, monitor, and release the people required to deliver the approved scope and schedule.

The plan translates the approved WBS and Integrated Project Schedule into a resource model that supports:

- Workstream ownership
- Capacity planning
- Responsibility assignment
- Cross-functional coordination
- Resource conflict management
- Specialist-resource planning
- Vendor planning
- Cost estimating and budgeting
- Performance monitoring
- Transition and resource release

All roles, allocations, locations, and organizational assumptions in this case study are synthetic.

---

## 2. Resource Strategy

Project Atlas will use a blended resource model consisting of:

### Core Delivery Team
A 14-member cross-functional team assigned directly to project delivery across project management, business analysis, architecture, application development, integration and data migration, validation, DevOps / release, and change readiness.

### Extended Business & Governance Team
Part-time stakeholders who provide sponsorship, business ownership, compliance, security, operations, regional subject-matter expertise, and enterprise integration support.

### Specialist External Support
Selected specialist services may be procured for activities such as independent security assessment, regulated validation support, or specialized technical review.

The core team size represents named delivery capacity. Extended stakeholders and external specialists are not counted within the 14-member core team.

## 5. Resource Acquisition & Mobilization

Resource mobilization will follow the approved schedule.

### Early Mobilization
Project Manager, Business Analysis Lead, Solution Architect, and selected business / governance stakeholders are required during initiation and requirements planning.

### Design & Build Ramp-Up
Application Development and Integration / Data Migration resources ramp up as requirements and solution design mature.

### Test & Validation Ramp-Up
Validation and QA resources increase allocation before formal testing and remain heavily engaged through validation, UAT, production readiness, and deployment.

### Change & Deployment Ramp-Up
Change / Training and DevOps / Release capacity increases as the project approaches UAT, readiness, cutover, and go-live.

### Closure Ramp-Down
Technical delivery capacity reduces after stabilization, while Project Management and business / operations roles remain engaged through handover and closure.

## 6. Capacity Planning

Monthly capacity is maintained in the Resource & RACI workbook.

Allocation percentages represent the share of each individual's working capacity planned for Project Atlas during the relevant month.

For example:

- **100%** = fully allocated to Project Atlas
- **50%** = approximately half of available working capacity
- **20%** = targeted part-time contribution

The capacity plan is intended for project-level planning and does not represent payroll or timesheet data.

Resource allocation will be reviewed against:

- Schedule demand
- Concurrent work packages
- Critical-path activities
- Defect / remediation demand
- Planned leave
- Competing organizational priorities
- Specialist-resource availability

---

## 7. Capacity Management Principles

The following planning principles apply:

1. No individual will be deliberately planned above 100% monthly project allocation.
2. Critical-path work receives priority when capacity conflicts occur.
3. Specialist resources are scheduled against defined windows rather than assumed continuously available.
4. Resource constraints that threaten milestones are recorded in the RAID Register.
5. Material resource changes affecting schedule, cost, or scope require impact analysis.
6. Resource forecasts are updated as schedule forecasts change.
7. Remaining capacity below 100% may support BAU or other organizational work and is not automatically charged to Project Atlas.

## 8. Roles & Responsibilities

### Project Manager
Owns integrated planning, delivery coordination, governance, scope / schedule / cost control, RAID, change control, stakeholder communication, vendor coordination, release readiness, and closure.

### Business Analysis Lead
Owns requirements elicitation, documentation, traceability, business clarification, acceptance criteria, and business coordination.

### Solution Architect
Owns solution architecture, design integrity, security / operational design coordination, and technical decision support.

### Application Development Lead
Owns application implementation, developer coordination, engineering quality, defect remediation, and release-candidate readiness.

### Application Developers
Develop and configure application components, execute developer verification, and resolve defects.

### Integration & Data Migration Lead
Owns integration design / delivery, migration strategy, reconciliation, and cross-system technical coordination.

### Integration / Data Engineers
Develop integrations and migration routines, support reconciliation, and resolve data / interface defects.

### Validation & Deployment Lead
Owns test and validation strategy, test governance, UAT coordination, release readiness, deployment coordination, hypercare, and transition.

### QA / Validation Analysts
Prepare and execute test / validation evidence, maintain defect and traceability information, and support UAT and release readiness.

### DevOps / Release Engineer
Supports environments, deployment automation / execution, configuration, release packaging, production deployment, monitoring readiness, and rollback execution.

### Change & Training Lead
Owns business readiness, stakeholder communications, training materials, training delivery, adoption preparation, and readiness tracking.

## 9. Responsibility Assignment

A RACI matrix is maintained for major project deliverables and decisions.

### RACI Definitions

- **R — Responsible:** Performs or coordinates the work.
- **A — Accountable:** Owns the outcome and final approval / decision.
- **C — Consulted:** Provides required input before completion or decision.
- **I — Informed:** Receives relevant information.

The project aims to maintain one clear accountable role for each major deliverable or decision wherever practical.

---

## 10. Cross-Functional Coordination

Cross-functional coordination will occur through:

### Weekly Delivery Review
Core workstream leads review progress, near-term capacity, blockers, dependencies, and upcoming milestones.

### Fortnightly Risk & Change Review
Resource constraints with material schedule, cost, or delivery impact are assessed alongside risks and proposed changes.

### Monthly Steering Committee
Major capacity risks, vendor constraints, budget impacts, and decisions requiring executive support are escalated.

### Stage-Gate Reviews
Required business, quality, security, operations, and technical resources participate in stage-gate decisions relevant to their authority.

---

## 11. Resource Conflict & Escalation

Resource conflicts will be handled in the following order:

1. Re-sequence non-critical work where float permits.
2. Rebalance capacity within the relevant workstream.
3. Use cross-skilled resources where appropriate.
4. Adjust lower-priority work outside Project Atlas where organizationally feasible.
5. Seek temporary specialist or vendor support.
6. Escalate unresolved critical resource constraints through project governance.

Changes that materially affect the approved schedule or cost baseline will follow integrated change control.

---

## 12. Team Performance & Working Practices

The Project Manager and workstream leads will support team effectiveness through:

- Clear ownership and acceptance criteria
- Regular delivery reviews
- Transparent issue escalation
- Decision logging
- Cross-workstream dependency management
- Constructive conflict resolution
- Knowledge sharing
- Recognition of delivery achievements
- Lessons learned throughout the project, not only at closure

The project operates across multiple regions; therefore, important decisions, actions, and approvals will be documented rather than relying only on verbal communication.

---

## 13. Resource Monitoring

Resource performance and demand will be monitored using:

- Monthly allocation %
- Planned FTE by month
- Workstream demand
- Schedule milestone demand
- Critical-path resource constraints
- Resource-related RAID items
- Forecast changes
- Vendor / specialist availability
- Resource-driven schedule variance

The resource plan will be revised when forecast demand changes materially.

---

## 14. Resource Release

Resources will be released progressively based on completion and transition needs.

- Business Analysis capacity reduces after requirements / UAT stabilization.
- Architecture capacity reduces after design and major technical risk resolution.
- Development and integration capacity reduce after production stabilization.
- QA / validation capacity remains through validation, release evidence, and hypercare.
- DevOps / Release remains through deployment and transition.
- Change / Training remains through business-readiness completion.
- Project Management remains through final closure and benefits handover.

Key knowledge and open items must be transferred before resource release.

---

## 15. Relationship to Cost Planning

This Resource Management Plan intentionally does not include salary or commercial rate data.

The next cost-planning step will apply synthetic rate assumptions to:

- Approved resource allocations
- Planned duration
- External specialist services
- Cloud and infrastructure
- Software and licensing
- Validation / security services
- Training and operational costs
- Contingency reserve

This allows the Cost Baseline to be derived from the approved scope, schedule, and resource plan rather than from an arbitrary top-down figure.
