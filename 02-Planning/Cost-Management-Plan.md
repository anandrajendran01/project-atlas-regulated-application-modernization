# Project Atlas — Cost Management Plan

## 1. Purpose

This Cost Management Plan defines how Project Atlas costs will be estimated, budgeted, funded, monitored, forecast, controlled, and closed.

The cost model is derived from the approved:

**Scope Baseline → Integrated Project Schedule → Resource Plan**

rather than from a standalone top-down budget.

All rates, commercial values, reserves, and financial assumptions in this case study are synthetic and created solely for portfolio demonstration.

---

## 2. Cost Management Objectives

Project Atlas will:

- Establish a traceable bottom-up project cost estimate.
- Aggregate estimates into control-account budgets.
- Establish a time-phased Cost Baseline.
- Maintain contingency reserve for identified project uncertainty.
- Maintain management reserve outside the Cost Baseline.
- Track actual cost, commitments, forecast, and variance during execution.
- Support future Earned Value Management using the approved Budget at Completion.
- Apply integrated change control to material changes affecting the approved baseline.

## 4. Estimating Approach

### 4.1 Labor / Delivery Resources

Labor cost is estimated using:

**Planned Monthly Allocation × Synthetic Loaded Monthly Rate**

The loaded planning rate represents a project-budget assumption rather than employee salary. It may conceptually include compensation, employer costs, contractor commercial rates, and applicable delivery overheads.

Rates vary by role and sourcing profile.

The approved Resource Plan provides the monthly allocation percentages used in the model.

### 4.2 External / Specialist Services

External services are estimated using planned specialist support windows for activities such as independent security assessment, regulated validation support, and targeted technical review.

### 4.3 Cloud & Infrastructure

Infrastructure cost includes project environments, testing capacity, migration / cutover requirements, temporary scaling, and production-transition support required during the project period.

### 4.4 Software & Licensing

Software and licensing costs represent project-specific platform, tooling, or incremental licensing assumptions required for delivery and testing.

### 4.5 Validation & Security Services

This category represents dedicated regulated-validation and security-assessment costs that are not already included within project labor.

### 4.6 Training & Change Readiness

This category includes user-readiness materials, training-delivery support, and selected change-readiness costs.

### 4.7 Travel & Operational Costs

This category provides a limited allowance for project-specific operational and cross-location activities.

## 6. Contingency Reserve

**Contingency Reserve: ₹22.00 lakh**

Contingency reserve is included within the Cost Baseline and therefore within BAC.

It is intended for identified uncertainty and known-unknowns, including exposure associated with:

- Integration complexity
- Data-quality remediation
- Additional validation effort
- Defect remediation
- Specialist support
- Release / cutover uncertainty

Use of contingency will be tracked through project cost control and linked to the relevant risk or issue where practical.

---

## 7. Management Reserve

**Management Reserve: ₹15.00 lakh**

Management reserve is:

- Outside the Cost Baseline
- Outside BAC
- Part of the Total Authorized Project Budget
- Reserved for unforeseen work within the overall project purpose
- Released only through appropriate management authorization

If management reserve is approved for use, the relevant amount will be transferred into the Cost Baseline through the approved governance process and the revised baseline will be documented.

## 10. Cost Control

During execution, the project will monitor:

- Planned Value / Cost Baseline
- Actual Cost
- Committed Cost
- Estimate to Complete
- Estimate at Completion
- Cost Variance
- Cost Performance Index
- Variance at Completion
- Contingency consumption
- Management-reserve usage
- Vendor commitments
- Forecast by month and control account

---

## 11. Cost Thresholds

For this portfolio case study, the following management thresholds are assumed:

- **≤ 3% control-account forecast variance:** Manage within the workstream where no milestone or scope impact exists
- **> 3% and ≤ 5%:** Project Manager review and corrective-action plan
- **> 5% control-account variance:** Steering Committee visibility
- **Projected BAC overrun:** Formal governance review required
- **Management reserve draw:** Executive / delegated governance authorization required
- **Material baseline revision:** Integrated change control required

These thresholds are synthetic.

---

## 12. Forecasting

Forecasts will be refreshed monthly using:

- Actual expenditure
- Open commitments
- Remaining resource demand
- Vendor forecasts
- Updated schedule forecast
- Risk exposure
- Approved changes
- Remaining contingency

A forecast change does not automatically change the approved baseline.

---

## 13. Relationship to Earned Value Management

The approved **₹240.00 lakh BAC** will become the financial baseline for future EVM reporting.

Planned Value will be derived from the time-phased Cost Baseline.

During the simulated execution phase, the portfolio will introduce:

- PV — Planned Value
- EV — Earned Value
- AC — Actual Cost
- CV — Cost Variance
- SV — Schedule Variance
- CPI — Cost Performance Index
- SPI — Schedule Performance Index
- EAC — Estimate at Completion
- ETC — Estimate to Complete
- VAC — Variance at Completion

EVM results will be added only after simulated project execution begins.

---

## 14. Financial Change Control

A proposed change affecting cost will include, where relevant:

- WBS impact
- Resource impact
- Schedule impact
- Direct cost impact
- Contingency impact
- Management-reserve requirement
- Benefits impact
- Funding impact
- Revised forecast
- Recommended decision

Approved baseline changes will be reflected consistently across the Scope Baseline, Schedule Baseline, Cost Baseline, forecast, and affected control artifacts.

---

## 15. Cost Closure

At project closure, the Project Manager will coordinate:

- Final actual-cost reconciliation
- Commitment and accrual closure
- Purchase-order / vendor closeout
- Remaining contingency reconciliation
- Management-reserve status
- Final forecast versus actual analysis
- Financial lessons learned
- Handover of post-project benefit measurement
