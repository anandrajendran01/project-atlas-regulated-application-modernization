# Project Atlas — Procurement Management Plan

## 1. Purpose

This plan defines how Project Atlas will plan, source, select, contract, govern, accept, and close external specialist services required for delivery.

Procurement is intentionally limited to services where external capability, independence, specialist knowledge, or temporary capacity adds value.

All vendors, commercial terms, selection scores, budgets, and dates in this case study are synthetic.

---

## 2. Make-or-Buy Approach

The core Project Atlas team retains ownership of project management, business analysis, architecture, application development, integration, migration, testing coordination, release management, and business readiness.

External procurement is used selectively for:

- Independent security assessment
- Specialist regulated-validation review / support
- Targeted integration / migration specialist capacity
- Performance / cutover technical support

The objective is to supplement—not replace—core project ownership.

## 4. Procurement Packages

### P-001 — Independent Security Assessment

**Purpose:** Independent vulnerability / security assessment before production readiness.

**Preferred Contract Type:** Fixed-price deliverable.

**Key Deliverables:**
- Assessment plan
- Testing / review execution
- Findings report
- Retest confirmation
- Final security-assessment closure evidence

### P-002 — Validation Review & Evidence Support

**Purpose:** Specialist support for regulated-validation planning, evidence review, and final validation readiness.

**Preferred Contract Type:** Fixed-price deliverable with defined review cycles.

**Key Deliverables:**
- Validation-plan review
- Traceability / evidence review
- Deviation-support review
- Validation-summary review
- Final specialist recommendations

### P-003 — Integration & Migration Specialist Support

**Purpose:** Temporary specialist capacity for complex integration / migration activities and troubleshooting.

**Preferred Contract Type:** Capped Time & Materials.

**Key Deliverables:**
- Agreed specialist support hours
- Technical recommendations
- Targeted integration / migration implementation support
- Issue-resolution support
- Knowledge transfer

### P-004 — Performance & Cutover Technical Support

**Purpose:** Specialized performance tuning and controlled cutover support around technical verification and production release.

**Preferred Contract Type:** Capped Time & Materials.

**Key Deliverables:**
- Performance analysis / tuning support
- Production-readiness recommendations
- Cutover technical support
- Hypercare transition support

## 5. Procurement Process

The procurement process will include:

1. Define need and make-or-buy rationale.
2. Develop Statement of Work / service requirements.
3. Confirm budget and required dates.
4. Identify qualified suppliers.
5. Conduct technical / commercial evaluation.
6. Complete security / compliance checks where required.
7. Obtain procurement and project approvals.
8. Execute contract / purchase order.
9. Mobilize supplier.
10. Monitor deliverables, cost, schedule, risk, and quality.
11. Accept deliverables against SOW criteria.
12. Close commercial obligations and document supplier performance.

---

## 6. Source Selection Criteria

For specialist suppliers, evaluation may consider:

- Technical capability
- Relevant regulated / enterprise experience
- Delivery capacity and schedule fit
- Commercial competitiveness
- Security, confidentiality, and data-handling controls

The workbook includes an example weighted vendor evaluation.

Lowest price alone will not determine supplier selection.

---

## 7. Statement of Work Principles

Each SOW will define:

- Scope of service
- Explicit exclusions
- Deliverables
- Required dates
- Customer dependencies
- Supplier responsibilities
- Acceptance criteria
- Reporting cadence
- Commercial model
- Change-control process
- Confidentiality / security obligations
- Invoice / payment milestones
- Closure requirements

---

## 8. Contract Type Rationale

### Fixed Price
Used where deliverables and acceptance criteria can be defined clearly and the supplier can reasonably price the work.

### Capped Time & Materials
Used where specialist support demand may vary but the project needs an upper commercial limit.

The contract type is selected based on uncertainty and ability to define the service—not simply by preference.

---

## 9. Supplier Governance

Supplier performance will be reviewed through:

- Mobilization confirmation
- Weekly / milestone delivery reviews as appropriate
- Deliverable acceptance
- Schedule adherence
- Quality of output
- Open actions / risks
- Commercial consumption
- Knowledge transfer
- Closure review

Vendor risks are included in the project RAID process where material.

---

## 10. Acceptance & Payment

Payments are linked to accepted deliverables or approved service consumption.

A deliverable is considered accepted when:

- SOW acceptance criteria are met.
- Required evidence is submitted.
- Review comments are resolved or dispositioned.
- Project / technical / quality authority confirms acceptance.
- No material contractual obligation remains outstanding for the milestone.

Acceptance authority varies by package and is defined in the Procurement Register.

---

## 11. Procurement Change Control

Changes to supplier scope, budget, deliverables, or timing require:

- Documented request
- Commercial impact
- Schedule impact
- Project baseline impact
- Risk impact
- Required approvals
- Contract / PO amendment where applicable

Supplier change does not bypass Project Atlas integrated change control.

---

## 12. Procurement Risks

Key procurement risks include:

- Late supplier onboarding
- Insufficient specialist availability
- Ambiguous SOW / acceptance criteria
- Commercial overrun under T&M arrangements
- Vendor knowledge dependency
- Security / confidentiality constraints
- Delayed deliverable acceptance
- Supplier schedule conflict

Mitigation includes early sourcing, capped commercial exposure, explicit acceptance criteria, backup options, and knowledge transfer.

---

## 13. Procurement Closure

Procurement closure includes:

- Final deliverable acceptance
- Final invoice / accrual reconciliation
- Open action transfer
- Knowledge-transfer confirmation
- Supplier-performance review
- Purchase-order / contractual closeout
- Procurement lessons learned

Project closure cannot assume vendor obligations are complete without commercial and deliverable confirmation.
