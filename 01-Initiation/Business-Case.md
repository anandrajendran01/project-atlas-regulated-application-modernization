# Project Atlas — Business Case
## 1. Executive Summary

The organization currently operates a legacy enterprise application supporting regulated business processes across multiple geographic regions.

The existing platform is increasingly expensive to maintain, relies on ageing technology, requires significant manual intervention, and has limited integration capability. These constraints increase operational effort, complicate system changes, and create growing technology and business-continuity risks.

Project Atlas proposes the modernization of the legacy platform through implementation of a supportable enterprise application with improved integration, workflow automation, validation traceability, and operational resilience.

The initiative requires a total authorized investment of **₹2.55 crore**, including a **₹2.40 crore cost baseline** and **₹15 lakh management reserve**.

## 2. Business Problem
The existing platform presents several challenges:
- Increasing maintenance and specialist-support requirements.
- Limited ability to integrate efficiently with modern enterprise systems.
- Manual activities across selected business workflows.
- Extended effort required for application changes and validation.
- Increasing operational dependency on legacy technology.
- Fragmented audit and traceability information.
- Growing business-continuity and technology-obsolescence risk.

Continuing with the existing platform is expected to increase maintenance complexity and constrain future business and technology initiatives.

## 3. Proposed Solution
Project Atlas will replace the existing application with a modern enterprise platform providing:

- Modernized application architecture.
- Improved business workflows.
- Integration with four enterprise systems.
- Controlled migration of required data.
- Improved system and validation traceability.
- Structured testing and business acceptance.
- Controlled production deployment.
- Formal operational handover and support transition.

## 4. Strategic Alignment
The initiative supports the organization's strategic objectives relating to:
- Technology modernization.
- Operational resilience.
- Process efficiency.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Reduced legacy-technology dependency.
- Improved data and systems integration.
- Sustainable application support.

## 5. Options Considered
| Option | Description | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Maintain Current Platform | Continue supporting the existing application with minimal change | Lowest immediate investment but retains growing technology, cost, and operational risks |
| Incremental Upgrade | Upgrade selected technical components without replacing the platform | Moderate investment but only partially addresses architectural and operational constraints |
| Modernize Platform | Replace the legacy application and associated workflows through Project Atlas | Higher initial investment but addresses technology, integration, supportability, and process limitations |

The modernization option is selected because it provides the strongest alignment with the longer-term technology and operational objectives.

## 6. Expected Benefits
### Financial Benefits
Expected recurring benefits include:
- Reduced legacy licensing and maintenance expenditure.
- Reduced specialist legacy-support requirements.
- Reduced operational effort through workflow automation.
- Lower incident-resolution and manual-reconciliation effort.

The case study assumes approximately **₹1.30 crore in annual quantified recurring benefits after stabilization**.

The estimated simple payback period is approximately **24 months after benefits reach steady-state levels**.

Detailed benefit calculations will be maintained separately within the Benefits Management documentation.

### Non-Financial Benefits
Expected non-financial benefits include:
- Improved system reliability.
- Improved user experience.
- Improved auditability.
- Stronger requirements and validation traceability.
- Reduced technology-obsolescence risk.
- Improved integration capability.
- Improved readiness for future business change.

## 8. High-Level Risks
Initial business-case risks include:
- Requirements complexity may exceed initial assumptions.
- Integration dependencies may delay testing.
- Data migration may identify unexpected quality issues.
- Validation effort may exceed planned estimates.
- Business-resource availability may affect UAT.
- Production cutover may require additional technical coordination.
- Vendor or specialist-resource availability may affect schedule commitments.

Detailed risks and response strategies will be developed in the Project RAID Register.

## 9. High-Level Schedule
The project is expected to complete within **nine months**, covering:

Initiation → Requirements → Design → Build → Integration Testing → Validation & UAT → Deployment → Hypercare → Closure

The detailed schedule will be baselined after scope and planning activities are completed.

## 10. Success Measures

Project Atlas will be considered successful when:
- Approved project scope has been delivered and formally accepted.
- Production deployment is completed within the approved project timeline.
- Project delivery remains within the approved cost baseline, subject to formally approved changes.
- No unresolved Severity-1 defects remain at go-live.
- Required validation and acceptance evidence is completed.
- Operational ownership has been successfully transitioned.
- Legacy-system retirement activities have been initiated as planned.
- Benefits measurement responsibility has been transferred to the appropriate business owner.

## 11. Recommendation
Proceed with Project Atlas subject to formal Project Charter approval, confirmation of project sponsorship, establishment of project governance, and completion of detailed scope, schedule, resource, risk, quality, and cost planning.
